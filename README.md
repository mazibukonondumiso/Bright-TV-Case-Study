# BRIGHT TV AUDIENCE ANALYTICS – DATA ANALYSIS PROJECT

# 1. Project Overview

The Bright TV Case Study focuses on analysing television audience viewing behaviour to understand how viewers interact with different TV channels, when they watch television and which audience groups contribute to viewing activity.

The purpose of this project is to transform raw viewing data into meaningful insights that can support better business decisions, audience understanding and content planning.

The analysis explores viewing patterns across different demographics, regions, TV channels, days of the week and times of the day.

The dataset contains 9,995 viewing records, representing 4,386 unique subscribers, covering the period from January to March 2016.

The project aims to answer important business questions, such as:

- Which TV channels attract the most viewing time?
- Which age groups contribute the most to TV viewing?
- What times of the day have the highest viewing activity?
- How does viewing behaviour differ between weekdays and weekends?
- Which regions contribute the most to television viewership?
- How does viewing activity change from month to month?

# 2. Methodology

The project followed a structured data analysis process to prepare, analyse and interpret the television viewing dataset.

# 2.1 Data Understanding

The first step involved exploring the dataset to understand its structure, available columns and the information contained in each field.

Key variables included:

- Subscriber ID
- Age and age groups
- Gender
- Ethnicity
- Region
- TV Channel
- Watch Date and Watch Time
- Day of the Week
- Month
- Time Buckets
- Watch Minutes
- Viewing Duration

# 2.2 Data Cleaning and Preparation

The dataset was examined and prepared to improve data consistency and ensure reliable analysis.

The preparation process focused on:

- Checking the dataset structure and column names.
- Identifying missing, blank and unknown values.
- Checking data consistency.
- Reviewing demographic categories.
- Preparing date and time fields for analysis.
- Using existing viewing duration and time classifications.
- Ensuring subscriber IDs could be used to identify unique viewers.

# 2.3 Data Analysis

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) was performed to identify patterns and trends in audience viewing behaviour.

The analysis focused on:

- Viewing activity by month.
- TV channel performance.
- Audience demographics.
- Viewing behaviour by region.
- Weekday and weekend comparisons.
- Viewing activity by time of day.
- Viewing duration classifications.

Key calculations included:

- Total Viewing Records: COUNT of records.
- Unique Viewers: DISTINCT COUNT of Sub_id.
- Total Viewing Minutes: SUM of Watch_minutes.
- Total Viewing Hours: SUM of Watch_minutes divided by 60.
- Average Viewing Minutes: AVERAGE of Watch_minutes.

# 2.4 Data Visualisation

-The analysis findings were presented using interactive dashboards and visualisations to make the results easier to understand.

-The visualisations include KPI scorecards, bar charts, column charts, line charts, donut charts and other relevant graphical representations.

-Interactive filters and slicers allow users to explore the data by different categories and compare viewing patterns.

# 3. Summary of Key Findings

The analysis revealed several important patterns in television audience behaviour.

# 3.1 Overall Viewing Activity

- The dataset contains 9,995 viewing records.
- A total of 4,386 unique subscribers were identified.
- Total viewing time was approximately 91,338.42 minutes, equivalent to 1,522.31 hours.
- Average viewing duration was approximately 9.14 minutes per record.

# 3.2 Monthly Viewing Trends

-March recorded the highest viewing activity, with 4,821 records and approximately 50,335 minutes of viewing time.

-February recorded 2,973 records, while January recorded 2,201 records.

-This indicates an increase in recorded viewing activity across the three months.

# 3.3 TV Channel Performance

-ICC Cricket World Cup 2011 recorded the highest total viewing time, with approximately 24,728 minutes.

-Live Events recorded the highest number of viewing records, with 1,770 records.

-Other channels with notable viewing activity included Channel O and Trace TV.

# 3.4 Audience Demographics

- The Youth age group recorded the highest number of viewing records, with 5,730.
- The Adult age group followed with 3,249 records.
- Male viewers accounted for the largest share of recorded viewing activity.

# 3.5 Regional Viewing Behaviour

-Gauteng recorded the highest viewing activity, with 3,652 records and approximately 33,214 viewing minutes.

-Western Cape and KwaZulu-Natal also contributed significantly to total viewing activity.

# 3.6 Viewing Behaviour by Time of Day

-Afternoon recorded the highest total viewing time, with approximately 21,052 minutes.

-Late Morning and Lunch Time also recorded notable viewing activity.

-This suggests that afternoon is an important period for audience engagement.

# 3.7 Weekday vs Weekend Viewing

Weekdays recorded 6,943 viewing records, compared to 3,052 records during weekends.

However, the average viewing duration per record was slightly higher on weekends than weekdays.

# 3.8 Viewing Duration

-The No Viewing classification accounted for the highest number of records, with 6,914 records.

-Long Viewing recorded 330 records, while Moderate Viewing recorded 425 records.

-These classifications provide an opportunity to investigate audience engagement and viewing duration further.

# 4. Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

# 4.1 Focus on High-Performing TV Channels

Bright TV should investigate the factors contributing to the strong viewing performance of ICC Cricket World Cup 2011 and Live Events.

These findings can help inform future content planning and channel programming decisions.

# 4.2 Improve Audience Engagement

The high number of records classified as No Viewing suggests an opportunity to investigate short or inactive viewing sessions.

Bright TV should explore ways to improve audience engagement and encourage longer viewing sessions where appropriate.

# 4.3 Optimise Programming Schedules

Since afternoon recorded the highest total viewing time, Bright TV should consider using this information when reviewing programme schedules and advertising opportunities.

# 4.4 Understand Different Audience Groups

The Youth and Adult age groups accounted for most viewing records.

Bright TV should continue analysing audience preferences to develop content that is relevant to different age groups.

# 4.5 Strengthen Regional Audience Analysis

Gauteng recorded the highest viewing activity.

Further regional analysis could help Bright TV understand differences in audience preferences and identify opportunities to grow viewership in other regions.

# 4.6 Monitor Viewing Trends

Bright TV should continue monitoring monthly, weekly and daily viewing patterns to identify changes in audience behaviour and evaluate channel performance over time.

# 4.7 Use Data-Driven Decision Making

The findings demonstrate the value of using audience data to understand viewing behaviour.

Bright TV can use interactive dashboards and regular reporting to monitor performance, identify trends and support informed business decisions.



# Conclusion

-The Bright TV Case Study demonstrates how data analytics can transform television viewing records into meaningful business insights.

-By analysing audience demographics, channel performance, viewing duration and time-based trends, the project provides a better understanding of television viewing behaviour. 

-The findings can support Bright TV in identifying audience patterns, evaluating channel performance and making more informed decisions about content planning and audience engagement.
